How the agents performed on each ticket, and why

#101 Tauhid Zaman's size S Bulldog tee: right result, highest cost

What the agents did Ticket 101 → Actual delegation flow

  • Boss → Inventory. The task already steered the agent: "Linked invoice 501 (Rush reprint CC-TEE-WHITE S)… verify… by asking Accounting as needed. Do not double-order if invoice 501 covers restock."
  • Inventory → Accounting. Accounting confirmed:
    • Invoice 501: "exists for $840.00 from Bulldog Print Co, due 2026-08-28, status open… 3 days overdue… amount paid is $0.00".
    • Restock: "The linked invoice itself covers the Rush reprint… do not request a duplicate purchase".
  • Inventory's answer. It reported its own findings, "size S has 0… Aisle B… M 5, L 3, XL 2… Bulldog Print Co… 5-day lead time… earliest possible restock date is 2026-09-05", plus Accounting's.
  • Boss → Customer Service. The task listed the verified facts. Customer Service wrote a draft offering M (5), L (3), XL (2) at $28, with an estimated restock on September 5.
  • Boss, final step. It called update_ticket(101, "escalated") with the note "…it covers the reprint, so no duplicate purchase… Escalated for human invoice-payment approval". Then King approved the $840, recorded as Payment #1. Cash tab

Why the result was right. The ticket links invoice 501, so the Boss passed its id down the chain, and the "no double restocks" shop rule made both specialists say no separate purchase was needed.

Why it cost the most. The same lookup was made twice 4 times Ticket 101 → MCP tools actually called:

  • get_desk(): Boss and Inventory.
  • get_cash_position(): Boss and Accounting.
  • check_stock("CC-TEE-WHITE","S",1): Inventory and Customer Service.
  • get_ticket(101): Accounting and Customer Service.

The cause is our own prompts:

  • Customer Service: its task literally said "Verified facts: size S stock 0 (shortfall 1), M 5, L 3, XL 2". But customer_service.md tells it to "Check stock and list price yourself with your tools, even if the task text includes numbers".
  • Everyone: shop_rules.md tells every agent to "call get_desk" for today's date.
  • Accounting: it fetched cash because Inventory's task asked for "cash-relevant facts", even though the Boss fetched cash too.

Gap shown in the flow. Customer Service's Answer is just "Draft for a human to review and send; no message has been sent." The draft itself is in a separate field, so the Boss's summary only says a draft exists.

Drafts (101, 103) Customer Service's tasks already contained every number in its drafts. It re-fetched stock and price anyway because its prompt tells it to. On #103 the Boss even handed it the $22 unit cost. The separate agent didn't actually keep cost data away from it. Only its prompt did.

Where having separate agents did prove useful in these runs:

  • Only the Boss writes. update_ticket appears only under the Boss in all 3 MCP tables. Only King's approvals moved money (Payments #1 and #2 in the Cash tab).
  • A second check before money moved. On #101, Accounting checked invoice 501 itself before the $840 was paid.
  • The audit shows who said what. For example, it's clear Inventory, not the Boss, recommended "no separate purchase".

Where it cost us in these runs: 7 handoffs, 31 model calls and 122,406 tokens for 3 tickets, with 7 lookups made twice.

Better fit for this app: one desk agent for tickets like #102 and #103, which reads get_ticket_context and escalates. Bring in a specialist only when money is about to move on a linked invoice, like #101's check_invoice(501).

We did not run a single-agent version. These conclusions come from what get_ticket_context returns, not from a measured comparison.
